# k=10 measurement — 2026-08-08 · `gpt-5.1` · prompt v6

The first measurement in this project that is a distribution rather than a point.
110 extractions, 734k tokens, 22 minutes. Raw runs are on disk and re-gradable.

## The gate is a coin flip

| | median | min | max |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PIPE-3 required-item recall | 67% | 64% | **76%** |
| PIPE-4-class criticals | 25 | 15 | **35** |
| round-trip checks passed | 9 | **7** | **10** |

Run by run: 9 · 8 · 8 · 7 · 10 · 9 · 10 · 9 · 8 · 9.

Nothing changed between those runs. Same prompt, same corpus, same decode
parameters, same commit. **A CI gate demanding 10/10 fails eight times in ten; a
gate demanding ≥8 fails one time in ten.** Either way the signal it emits is
mostly about which sample it drew, and a gate that fails at random is a gate a
team learns to ignore.

Recall spans **11.1 points** across identical configurations. Every prompt
comparison made on 2026-08-07 — v3 vs v4 vs v5 vs v6, each a single run — moved
less than that. None of them measured anything.

## Where the 89 required items actually live

| | count | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| always extracted | 40 | the stable core — gateable |
| never extracted | 13 | real, reproducible gaps |
| flickering | 36 | neither passing nor failing |

**Only 40 of 89 items are stable enough to carry a threshold.** The 13 permanent
misses are the honest work list. The 36 in between are not noise to be averaged
away — under PIPE-15 they are the product's uncertainty signal, and the framework
already says what to do with them: a fact appearing in under 70% of runs should
arrive hedged or as a DISAMBIGUATE, not asserted.

Six items sit at 10–20%: `dom:self:education/senior`,
`hardship:maya/concern/parkinson`, `secondhand-chain:leon/goal/atlanta`,
`group-ramble:ama/relation/tunde`, `group-ramble:kevin/goal/woodworking`, and the
`woodworking` thread. These are close to stable misses and should be read as
such.

## PERF-5, first measurement

PERF-5 budgets ≤ 20s p95 for a 3-minute memo. Scoped to memos of ≤ 450 words
(~3 minutes of speech): **median 7.3s, p95 20.3s — over by 2%.** Zero retries
across 110 extractions.

But the budget indexes the wrong variable. `group-ramble` is 182 words — the
fourth *shortest* memo — and the second *slowest* at 21.8s, because it contains
eight people and produces a large payload. `eliah` at 1,847 words takes 45.7s.
**Latency tracks output complexity, not audio length.** A budget stated per
minute of audio will keep mis-predicting; it should be stated per unit of
extracted content, or simply measured against the honest-progress-UI requirement
it already carries.

## PIPE-4, first real denominator

`adjudicate.py` Stage A — mechanical support checks, no judge yet:
**90.0% structural precision** on a single run, against ◊ ≥ 97%. That is a
*ceiling*: it counts only claims that fail structurally (unresolvable subject,
quote absent from the transcript, hedge dropped) and defers every semantic
question. The reference fixtures score 100% on the same check, which is what a
hand-authored fixture should do and is another reason not to read them as a
baseline.

## What this does not measure

The grader is still the one described in MEASUREMENT-REWORK Part 1.A. PIPE-4
counts enumerated forbidden items; required facts match by substring plus exact
predicate. The numbers above are precise measurements of a biased instrument —
which is exactly why collection was separated from grading. These 110 runs
re-grade for free when Phase 2 lands.
