# v7 vs v6 — the first paired prompt comparison

Two k=10 collections, 220 extractions, compared item-by-item rather than as two
averages. This is the first prompt comparison in the project that could have
detected anything, and the answer is more interesting than "better".

## The targeted rules worked, and worked hard

Each v7 rule was written against a specific measured failure. All three hit:

| required item | v6 | v7 | rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `secondhand-chain: leon/goal/atlanta` | 10% | **100%** | 35 |
| `eliah: hedge "I think I got a lot closer"` | 0% | **90%** | 37 |
| `eliah: hedge "I want to say"` | 0% | **70%** | 36 |
| `group-ramble: kevin/goal/woodworking` | 10% | **60%** | 35 |
| `group-ramble: thread woodworking` | 10% | **60%** | 35 |

The Atlanta result is the one to look at. It did not merely stop claiming Leon
lives there — the fact was **recategorised into the goal it always was**, from
10% to 100%. That is the ideal shape of a fix: not suppression, but the claim
landing in the right place.

FN-40 directly: residence assertions fell from **51 to 17** across ten runs
(median 5 → 1). The wrong ones are gone — Leon/Atlanta 9/10 → 0, Ama/Chicago
8/10 → 0, Roger/Pacific-Northwest 4/10 → 0, Jen/Berkeley 6/10 → 1. Tunde/Oakland
survives at 6/10 and should: *"it's his new place in Oakland"* is a residence.

## The aggregate is a wash, and that is the honest headline

| | v6 | v7 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| recall, median (min–max) | 66.7% (64.4–75.6) | **70.0%** (67.8–74.4) |
| criticals, median (min–max) | 25 (15–35) | **15** (12–17) |
| paired items improved | | 21 |
| paired items regressed | | 20 |
| sign test | | **p = 1.000** |

Twenty-one items improved and twenty regressed. **The net change in recall is not
distinguishable from noise**, and the only reason we know that is the pairing —
the medians alone (66.7% → 70.0%) would have read as a clean win, and on a single
run each it would have looked like a triumph.

Criticals are the real aggregate gain: median 25 → 15, and the range collapsed
from 15–35 to 12–17. Fewer bad claims *and* far less variance.

## What it cost

| regression | v6 | v7 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `eliah: self location/167th` | 60% | **10%** |
| `hardship: parkinson thread` | 90% | **60%** |
| `eliah: eliah/employment/google` | 60% | 30% |
| `eliah: self employment/microsoft` | 60% | 30% |
| `nikos: entity picnic` | 30% | 0% |

Two are worth naming:

**Rule 35 overcorrected.** "I lived on 167th and Grand Concourse" is a residence
by any reading, and v7 drops it nine times in ten. The rule taught the model to
distrust place-mentions, and it now distrusts the good ones too.

**The hardship thread degraded, and nothing in v7 touches hardship.**
`condition_hardship` went from 10/10 runs to 6/10 — not misclassified, absent
entirely. Maya's father's Parkinson's simply is not tracked in four runs out of
ten. INV-20 is not violated (a thread that does not exist raises no prompts), so
this is recall rather than safety, but it is the highest-stakes content in the
corpus and it got worse from an unrelated edit.

## The pattern underneath: the prompt is accreting

| | lines | words | rules |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| v1 | 65 | 621 | 15 |
| v3 | 131 | 1,335 | 20 |
| v6 | 244 | 2,377 | 34 |
| v7 | 295 | 2,872 | 37 |

Nearly five times the v1 prompt, and the marginal rule now appears to **trade one
item for another** — 21 up, 20 down is what dilution looks like. Each rule works
on the case it was written for while competing for attention with the other
thirty-six.

That is a hypothesis, not a finding: this comparison changed three rules at once
and cannot separate "rule 35 caused the hardship regression" from "the prompt got
longer". The experiment that would settle it is cheap now that the harness
exists — run v6 with *only* rule 35 appended, and v7 with the oldest rules pruned,
and pair both against these collections.

## What to do

1. **Keep v7.** The targeted wins are large and reproducible, criticals are down
   with much tighter variance, and no constitutional check regressed.
2. **Narrow rule 35** so a first-person "I lived at X" survives it.
3. **Investigate the hardship regression before it is inherited.** It is
   unexplained and it is the worst category to lose.
4. **Test the dilution hypothesis** — the accretion cannot continue indefinitely
   and this is the first evidence it has a cost.
